# Composition grammar (the second reference sheet)

Target reference: the storyboard of eight frames sent 25 Nov, 05:37 Dhaka. Photoreal natural world, one
impossible object in it, shot like a film still.

## The frame grammar, per chapter

Every frame is three layers, in this order of importance:

1. **Near ground, bottom edge, slightly out of focus.** Dense and specific: grass blades, daisies,
   pebbles, moss, sand ripples, coral. This is the layer that makes it read as photographed rather
   than modelled, and it is the one the early passes had none of.
2. **Mid ground, the subject.** The pearl sits in this band, roughly a third of the frame width, with
   its trail leading back and up out of frame.
3. **Distance, layered.** A mountain or cloud line, then a haze band, then sky. Two or three distinct
   values so depth reads as distance, not as fog.

Camera: low, close to the ground, wide, level or slightly up. Horizon sits high in the frame, above
the middle, so the near ground gets real estate.

The orb: a glass pearl, not a glow ball. Refraction, a bright rim, a highlight, and a thin trail of
light. Small in frame, never the largest thing.

## Component library

Each of these is one small thing, sourced, checked on its own before it goes in a scene. No component
is authored: it comes from Megascans, or from the water studies in the owner's starred list.

- ground: grass clump, daisy cluster, pebble scatter, moss patch, sand ripple, snow drift
- rock: small boulder, cliff chunk, slate shard, ice chunk, iceberg
- plant: fern, dead trunk, pine, birch, palm, kelp blade, coral fan, anemone
- atmosphere: cloud sprite, light shaft, bubble, dust mote, haze band
- the pearl and its trail

Megascans 3D plants are often FBX only. Textures and materials come as a 2K texture set: albedo,
normal, roughness, AO, gloss, displacement. Take the glTF low when it exists.
